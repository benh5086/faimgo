# Freelancing Your Skill — Your 30/60/90 Playbook

*A playbook to follow, not a book to read. Seven short plays. Do one at a time — don't read ahead.*

---

## Start where you are (10 seconds)

Pick the line that sounds like you. It tells you where to jump in — you don't have to do every play from the top.

- **"I've got the skill, I just can't find customers."** → Start at **Play 4**. Skim Plays 1–3 to tighten your offer and price, then focus your energy on finding and reaching people.
- **"I can sell — I'm just not sure what to offer."** → Start at **Play 1** and spend real time there. Once your offer is sharp, the rest moves fast for you.
- **"I know the basics but I'm stuck actually starting."** → Start at **Play 1** and do **one play a week**. Don't skip. This is the most common place to be, and the most fixable — you don't need more knowledge, you need to run the plays in order.
- **"I'm starting from scratch."** → Read *The one idea* below, then Play 1. Go slower; the order never changes.

---

## The one idea (read once, then keep it in your head)

You're a business with **zero history**. A buyer can't see reviews or a track record, so hiring you feels **risky**. Everything that works early does one thing: **it makes saying yes feel safe.** Not cheaper — *safer*. That single idea explains every play below.

---

## The map

Plays 1–5 are your **first 30 days** (land your first client). Play 6 is **days 31–60** (make it repeat). Play 7 is **days 61–90** (grow it). One play at a time.

---

## Play 1 — Your one offer
*Vague is invisible. Specific feels safe.*

- [ ] Write it in one line: **"I help [specific person] get [specific outcome] by [what you do]."**
- [ ] Draft five versions. Keep the one that makes a *real* person nod.

**Done when:** you can say it in one breath, and it names a specific person and a specific outcome.

## Play 2 — Price it
*Cheap reads as risky. Win on safety, not on being the cheapest.*

Two tempting prices are both traps: **copying a competitor** (they're charging for history you don't have yet) and **going rock-bottom cheap** (too cheap makes a buyer wonder what's wrong). Instead:

- [ ] Set your price **~20–30% below the going rate** — a small nudge to take a chance on you, not your selling point.
- [ ] Add **one risk-killer**: free revisions, three options to pick from, or a guarantee (e.g. a longer-lasting finish on a product). Guarantee away the thing they're afraid of.
- [ ] Offer **at most three simple tiers.** More than three adds friction, and friction reads as risk.
- [ ] Structure payment to protect both sides: a **30–40% deposit** to start, or **80% before the final step**.

**Done when:** you have a price with at least one built-in guarantee.

## Play 3 — Set up the minimum to be hireable
*You need three things. None of them is a website.*

- [ ] **One page that shows you're real:** a clean Notion page or Google Doc — your offer, 2–3 examples (or one strong sample you make today), and how to reach you.
- [ ] **A way to get paid:** Wave (free), or a PayPal/Stripe payment link.
- [ ] **A one-line way to be reached.** That's it. Building a website now is procrastination dressed up as work.

**Done when:** you have a link you could paste to a stranger and a way to invoice them.

## Play 4 — Find the right person
*Sell to the person who feels the pain — not just the one who signs the check.*

- [ ] Aim at the **person who actually feels the problem** (the one using it or building with it), not just whoever pays. Their say-so drives the deal.
- [ ] Build a list of **~20 real prospects** who fit. Start with anyone you have a connection to, then add cold ones from LinkedIn, Google Maps, or directories.

**Done when:** you have 20 names and you know, for each, *who* feels the pain.

## Play 5 — Reach out, then close
*People buy when they feel understood, not when they feel sold to.*

- [ ] **Tailor every message** to that one person's situation. Generic outreach is invisible — the reader knows it went to a hundred people.
- [ ] Use AI as your unfair advantage: have it **research each prospect and draft a personalized opener** fast. You pick the right person and the real pain; it speeds up the words.
- [ ] **Send 10 personalized messages this week.** Ten specific beats two perfect. Track them: name, how you know them, date, reply, next step.
- [ ] **Follow up once** a few days later — most replies come from the follow-up.
- [ ] When someone's interested, **don't pounce.** Mirror it back first: *"So what you're really after is X, by this date, without Y — right?"* Then confirm outcome, price, and timeline, and send the invoice.

**Decode the real question:** if they ask for *"a more competitive option,"* they're asking for a **lower price**. Answer with framed value — an add-on, a small first-project discount, a price-match — not a naked "okay, it's cheaper now."

**Done when:** you've earned your first dollar — or you have live conversations and a clear reason why not (usually: pitch more, or reach a different person).

> **If you sent 10 and got zero replies:** it's almost always the *list* (wrong people) or the *offer* (too vague to feel safe) — not you. Fix one, send 10 more.

## Play 6 — Days 31–60: Make it repeat
*One project is a fluke. A system is a business — and now you have a little history. Use it.*

- [ ] Ask every client two things: **"Would you leave a short testimonial?"** and **"Who else do you know who needs this?"** A testimonial borrows their credibility to lower the next buyer's risk.
- [ ] **Turn one-offs into a retainer** where it fits ("I'll handle X every month for $Y"). Predictable income, less selling.
- [ ] **Raise your price for the next client** — you have proof now. Nudge it up until you feel mild resistance; that's your real ceiling.
- [ ] **Keep pitching 5 a week even while busy.** Stopping outreach when you get busy is what causes the feast-and-famine cycle.

**Done when:** you have one testimonial, one referral or repeat, and a higher rate than you started with.

## Play 7 — Days 61–90: Grow it (on purpose)
*Shape it to fit your life.*

- [ ] **Productize** into a named package with fixed scope and price ("The Listing Refresh — 5 descriptions, 3-day turnaround, $X").
- [ ] **Pick one channel** — the one your best client came from — and go deeper. Don't spread across five.
- [ ] **Decide your ceiling deliberately:** keep it a tidy side income, or push to replace a salary. Both are valid — choose, don't drift.
- [ ] **Protect your time by raising prices, not adding hours.**

**Done when:** you have steady income you could keep running, and a clear decision about whether to keep it small or grow it.

## Templates (copy, personalize — never send as-is)

**Your offer:** "I help [specific person] get [specific outcome] by [what you do]."

**First outreach:** Hi [Name] — I came across [specific thing about them] and noticed [genuine observation about their problem]. I help [their type] with [outcome]; I even sketched [a quick idea] for your situation. Want me to send it over? No pressure either way.

**Follow-up (once):** Hi [Name] — floating this back up in case it got buried. Happy to share that [idea] whenever it's useful.

**Mirror a buying signal:** Just so I get it exactly right — what you're really after is [their outcome], by [timeline], without [the headache they mentioned]. Is that the heart of it?

**Answer "can you do it cheaper?":** I can make this work for you. Rather than just cutting the price, here's what I'd do: [add-on / three options / small first-project discount]. You get [more value], and it stays something I can deliver at the quality you actually want.

**Confirm the work:** Here's the plan: [outcome], by [date], for [price], including [revisions / guarantee]. I'll send an invoice for the [30–40%] deposit to start. Anything you'd add?

**Ask for testimonial + referral:** Really glad that worked out. Two quick things — a short line I could use as a testimonial? And anyone else who could use the same thing?

## Common mistakes

Pricing too cheap to seem safe (it does the opposite). Copying an established competitor's price. Pitching the person who pays instead of the one who feels the pain. Blasting one message to everyone. Rushing to sell the moment someone shows interest. Answering the literal question instead of the real one. Building a website instead of reaching out. Stopping outreach when you get busy.
