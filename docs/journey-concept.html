The journey — and the glue between every step

Every customer gets their own version of this. What matters here isn't the steps — it's the glue between them, so no one ever finishes one thing and wonders "wait, where am I now?"

The rule that kills "where am I?": every step ends by pointing at the next one and saying why. No screen ever just stops — you finish a move and get handed the next, with the reason.
1

Take the assessment

A few branching questions, about two minutes. Your answers pick your path, your priority (fast / big / easy), and where you're stuck.

Glue

→ Before anything else, we reflect you back: "Here's what we heard — you're great at X, you want Y, you're stuck on Z." The first moment of "they get me."

2

Meet your map

Your personal route lights up. The other roads are there but quiet. You see the whole shape — once.

Glue

→ Then it zooms in. The map narrows to the ONE thing to do first — so you're never left staring at the whole graph.

3

Your first move — a quick win

One small, near-guaranteed win (the junction you just saw). You do it.

Glue

→ The moment you finish, we celebrate it, then ask one light question: how did that go?

4

The check-in — the hinge

One tap, plus an optional note. This is where the system both tracks your progress and learns more about you — so your map gets sharper every loop. It's not fixed at the assessment; it's alive.

Glue

→ Because of your answer, your next move appears — and your pin moves forward on the map. You see the progress.

5

The next move — and the loop

Move → check-in → next move. Every step hands you the next, with the reason. Sometimes a check-in reveals a better road…

Glue · reroute

↘ …and we reroute you — "because you built a reusable template, a bigger road just opened." Even the plot-twists come with a why, so they feel earned, not random.

6

Milestones

First dollar. First repeat customer. We zoom out: "Three weeks ago you were here — look now." Invisible progress, made visible.

Underneath all of it, the map is always there, and the pin always says "you are here." That persistent anchor is why the whole thing feels like one continuous journey — not a pile of disconnected tasks.

Concept only — to show the shape of the flow and where the connective tissue lives. You'll test the real thing and tell us where a transition still feels abrupt.
